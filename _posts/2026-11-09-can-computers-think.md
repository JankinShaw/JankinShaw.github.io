In *MINDS, BRAINS AND SCIENCE*, based on his 1984 BBC Reith lecture, John Searle talked about computer and brain and how computers work. 

In the second chapter, he starts to illustrate on how computers think. He pointed out that the prevailing idea or stance in "philosophy, psychology and artificial intelligence" is that human brains are like computers, and the most extreme version, termed 'strong AI' by Searle, thereof says, 'the mind is to the brain, as the program is to the computer hardware.' (p.28)

What does it stylistically mean by saying *A to B as C to D*? It means an 'analogy', formally

$$A:B::C:D$$

I want to add a little bit more words about *analogy*. So every time we use analogy, we're trying to make sense of one thing or one pair of relation based on another pair of relations that we already have enough knowledge of. So for example, it would be meaningless to say student to university is like policeman to police station, where both set of relations are clearly defined and everybody knows sort of the nature of these relationships, right? Students, the ten university study in university, policemen work in police station. Also, these are sort of, if we use a more upper level abstraction, we could use something like affiliation, right? Students is affiliated to the university and policemen is affiliated to the police station. And in this case, analogous would make very little contribution to adding our understanding of either relations. And we even aren't sure what would be the targeted relationship that we are trying to make more sense of. 

And why is it really important here? Because without knowing this, if we see something like 'the mind is to the brain, as the program is to the computer hardware.' (p.28). If both relationships are presumed to be hitherto well understood, what are we trying to achieve here with this analogy? Are we saying that, mind - brain, program - computer software are related? But again, are we trying to understand mind-brain relationship more based on our understanding to program - computer hardware or the other way around? It’s not hard to see here that, for an *analogy* to work in order to add our understanding to something instead of being a pure poetic phrase, we would be nudged towards identifying or assuming a pair of relationship that we already know well of. And setting in stone another pair of relation that are not well understood so far, as far as not in the context.

This looks like a mechanistically styled paraphrase and added little new interpretive effect to the previous so-called 'standard biological approach', which states that mind and its inherent processes are caused by what's happening in the brain, i.e. brain processes. They are both basically trying to explain that be it the mind-brain or software-hardware relation, the first was empowered by the second entity. What’s the insight here?
